Home Inspection Red Flag Guidelines – Reference Document for AI Realtor

This document provides expert guidelines on identifying red flags in home inspection reports. Use it to evaluate inspection findings and advise home buyers.

FOUNDATION RED FLAGS

Foundation cracks wider than 1/4 inch are concerning. Horizontal cracks often indicate more serious structural issues than vertical or diagonal cracks. Stair-step cracks in brick or block foundations may suggest differential settlement. Look for signs of water intrusion: efflorescence (white mineral deposits), rust stains, or moisture stains along foundation walls.

ROOF RED FLAGS

Curled, cracked, or missing shingles indicate the roof is near or past its useful life. granule loss in gutters suggests advanced wear. Sagging roof deck may indicate structural problems or water damage. Improper flashing around chimneys, vents, and skylights can cause leaks. Multiple layers of shingles (especially 3+ layers) add excessive weight.

PLUMBING RED FLAGS

Polybutylene (gray plastic) piping is prone to failure and was used between 1978–1995. Galvanized steel pipes corrode from the inside and typically fail after 40–50 years. Evidence of past leaks: staining, soft spots, or repair patches. Low water pressure may indicate corroded pipes or undersized supply lines. Water heater age over 10 years—consider replacement budget.

ELECTRICAL RED FLAGS

Aluminum wiring (common in 1965–1973 homes) can overheat at connections. Federal Pacific or Zinsco panels have known safety issues. Ungrounded (2-prong) outlets throughout the home indicate outdated wiring. Double-tapped breakers (two wires on one breaker) may violate code. Fuse boxes suggest very old electrical systems.

HVAC RED FLAGS

Furnace or AC units over 15–20 years old are nearing end of life. Rust or corrosion inside the furnace heat exchanger can indicate carbon monoxide risk. Improper venting of combustion appliances is a safety hazard. Inadequate duct sizing or poor insulation affects efficiency. Evidence of refrigerant leaks in AC systems.

WATER DAMAGE AND MOLD

Recurring water stains on ceilings or walls suggest ongoing leaks. Musty odors often indicate hidden mold. Soft, spongy floors near water sources may indicate rot. Peeling paint or bubbling wallpaper can signal moisture intrusion. Evidence of past flooding: high-water marks, sump pump installation.

STRUCTURAL AND SAFETY

Bowing or leaning walls may indicate foundation or framing issues. Load-bearing wall modifications without proper support. Stair railings that are too short or lack proper baluster spacing. Deck ledger boards not properly attached to the house. Missing or inadequate smoke and CO detectors.

SEPTIC AND DRAINAGE

Wet spots or odors in the yard may indicate septic failure. Trees planted over or near drain fields can damage the system. Improper grading causing water to flow toward the foundation. Gutters draining too close to the foundation instead of 4–6 feet away.
